**One-line law:** Every hero message has **one big idea**. Everything else only exists to reinforce that idea. Stacking many unrelated “great things” is **toss salad copy** — and it loses.

- Built Agora’s acquisition machine: teams of copywriters generating demand with words.

## The anti-pattern — toss salad copy

What most operators / copywriters do:

> Throw every great bullet, benefit, proof, and angle into one message.  
> “If that one doesn’t work, one of them will.”

Masterson’s name for it: **toss salad copy** — jumble everything, hope something sticks.

**Sharp Flow smell:** tipster menu posts · “6 reasons + 4 wallets + weekend slate + SUMMER + vault” in one hero · dual-ask · scoreboard dump with no spear tip.

---

## The Rule of One

Center the message on **one big idea** — the tip of the spear.

### Three tests for the one big idea

| Test | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Easy to understand** | Stranger gets it in one pass — no decode required |
| **Easy to believe** | Feels true / earned — not tipster fantasy |
| **Interesting or unique** | Counterintuitive, scarce, or new to them — not NPC noise |

All three → a message people take **one step toward you**.

### What sits under the one idea

Sub-bullets / stories / facts / arguments are **not** alternate pitches.  
They exist **only** to reinforce the singular argument.

| Toss salad | Rule of One |
|------------|-------------|
| “Six morning strategies” that are unrelated | One core benefit — six subpoints that all prove *that* benefit |
| 15 crazy headline ideas competing | One USP / one selling proposition |
| Many proofs fighting for attention | Proofs that all serve the same claim |

**USP law:** one core selling proposition everyone can immediately understand, that is counterintuitive, and believable — not 19 propositions.

**SPCL is not that proposition.** Status/Power/Credibility/Likeness prove the one idea. They are not a substitute for picking it.

---

## Why picking only one is hard (and good)

When you may only pick one, you are forced to ask:

> Is this the **best** idea?  
> Is this the most centralized, focused tip of the spear?

That selection pressure is the point. The hard part is choosing the one idea. Once chosen, the sales message is transparent and simple to write.

Hormozi’s result after keeping Rule of One top-of-mind: copy becomes **more targeted, crisper, more central** — one idea the reader must be convinced of.

**Empirical check:** best-converting ads you’ve ever run are usually the simplest, most direct, clearest.

## Sharp Flow / X — how we use this

### Every hero / soft-ask / QT

1. **Name the one big idea** in plain English before drafting (one sentence).
2. Run the three tests (understand / believe / unique).
3. Line 1 + media + close must all serve **that** idea — SPCL stamps are *how we prove it*, not a second menu of ideas.
4. Kill any bullet that introduces a **competing** claim (second offer, second product room, second flex).

### Examples (shape, not copy)

| One big idea | Reinforcing only |
|--------------|------------------|
| Proven wallets sized *this* play before the public pile | $ / Nx usual / L30 / say–do — all prove that claim |
| We sat the valley and the book is green under | −units → V12 → green chart — one persistence arc |
| Soft ask: grade the board yourself | One proof trail → one invite — not SUMMER + vault + weekend slate |

### Kill list (Rule of One violations)

- Toss salad heroes (many unrelated benefits)
- Dual soft-asks in one post
- “Also…” product menus after the idea already landed
- Headline that sells A while body sells B
- SPCL letter-stamping as *content* instead of as *proof of the one idea*

---

## Agent checklist (before stage)

- [ ] One-sentence big idea written down
- [ ] Passes easy to understand / believe / interesting-or-unique
- [ ] Every line and media beat reinforces that idea only
- [ ] No toss-salad competing claims
- [ ] SPCL letters named as *proof stamps for this idea*, not separate pitches
