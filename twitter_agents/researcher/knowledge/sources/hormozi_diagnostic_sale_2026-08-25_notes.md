## Big idea

**Don’t sell the membership / the product / the one-off service.**  
Sell **current → desired**, name the **obstacle**, present **your vehicle** as the path, and **tie price to getting them there** (often with time + guarantee).  
Same delivery; different packaging/presentation → LTV and recurring explode (example: $200→$800 avg; one-off rebooks → packaged goal).

Bridge: **transactional product** (one widget) sold in a way that **feels custom** (diagnostic).

---

## Diagnostic process (customer-facing arc)

1. **Pre-sale questionnaire** — arm the seller; raise buyer’s awareness of the problem; agitate (how long, financial cost, 5-year worse).  
2. **Info + credit card on file** — “complete your profile / standard procedure” *before* the close so the ask isn’t for the card later.  
3. **Current** — where are you today? (ideally third-party: scale, chart they point at — *they* name the pain).  
4. **Desired** — where do you want to be? (**They** set the goal → price feels like *their* decision — Yogurtland / cup-on-scale effect).  
5. **Obstacle** — what’s between current and desired? Get **their words** so you can play them back. What they think is in the way often isn’t — but use their language.  
6. **Present** desired state + **your vehicle** to overcome the obstacle + **price tied to the path** (duration, steps, guarantee).  
7. **Prepay incentive** — benefit language (“prepay discount”), not salesman “pay in full.”

---

## Five implementation steps (operator)

1. **Secret shop** your own sales (you’ll be horrified).  
2. **Find the constraint** (here: offer/packaging — not changing core product).  
3. **Repackage** — sell the **solution/goal**, not the one-off.  
4. **Kill zombies** — anticipate objections *before* price; after price you’re “a salesperson.” Stay trusted expert; **childlike curiosity** (tilt head); **if you win the argument, you lose the sale**.  
5. **Implement** diagnostic process and train to autopilot (especially low-skill labor: force script via POS checkboxes).

## Gym example (sell the goal)

- Not: “$99/month membership with these features.”  
- Diagnostic: current weight → desired (e.g. 140) → gap ÷ safe rate = **weeks**.  
- Price = rate × weeks (e.g. $99/wk × 45 = $4,500).  
- Guarantee: follow steps (show up, log food) → work free until goal.  
- Translation: paying this means you can **count that weight gone** if you follow the steps.  
- Then: want to save a little? **Prepay** → 10% off → card on file. Done.

**Key:** They bought **getting to 140**, not a membership. Product unchanged.

## Medical/aesthetics chain (same pattern)

- Not buying filler/Botox one-off — buying a **look** / how people see them.  
- Chart: where are you now → how pretty do you want to be (**they** pick).  
- Expert maps path + time + package; price follows **their** before/after.  
- Old process: agitate ugly → one service → pay → leave (weak upsell).  
- New: package to goal → recurring/membership/payment plans.

**Results cited (attacked line):** recurring share 9%→60%; memberships 0→30%; visits 1 extra→4–6; ~$20k/mo→$250k/mo on that line; company locations 14→32 with sales process as lever.

## Price presentation (anchors + good guy)

Present **full-boat / payment-plan rate first** (high anchor), then **benefits for prepaying** — you are the good guy.  

**Don’t** present low cash price then pile on “interest if you need payments” (bank energy — everyone hates banks).

**Language:** “prepay discount” / “save if you prepay today” — never “pay in full” (seller benefit, not customer benefit).

### Sales step-downs (order)

1. Prepay (best discount)  
2. Third-party financing if useful  
3. Partial / half-down (smaller discount)  
4. Continuity / payment plan matched to delivery  
5. Smallest next commitment (“just book the next visit”) — **everyone buys something**

Mantra: everyone buys something.

**Upsell fractal note:** top 20% ≈ 5× spending power → upsell often ~5× current ticket if you want meaningful AOV lift; tiny product upsells are noise.

**Recurring bonus:** payment-plan framing + auto-recurring at end of plan can beat pure month-to-month churn psychology; naming matters to customer, cashflow regularity matters to business.

## Transactional vs custom

| | Transactional | Custom / diagnostic feel |
|--|---------------|---------------------------|
| Fit | Customer → fixed product | Path sized to their current→desired |
| Ticket / speed | Lower, faster | Higher feel, more ops complexity |
| Magic | One product, efficient | **Same product**, personalized *presentation* |

## Sharp Flow application (paywall / offer)

**They are not buying:** picks, a tip membership, “access to a dashboard.”  

**They are buying:**  
**Current** — guessing, stacked subs, finding out after tipoff, pressing noise.  
**Desired** — win more: sized up when proven money + conviction are real; flat/fade when losers are loud; notified at lock.  
**Obstacle** — no live view of proven $ / size vs usual / for-against / limits-steam / conviction ladder.  
**Vehicle** — Sharp Flow (unchanged delivery; packaging is the sale).  
**Price** — tied to that path (trial = risk reverse; weekly/monthly = vehicle to the goal, not “a membership”).

**Questionnaire energy on-page (without creepy card tricks in copy):**  
Agitate cost of becoming sharp · years of noise · missing size-ups · studying most-bet.  
Then: desired state + our board as vehicle + proof + trial.

**Kill zombies before price:** “Is this just picks?” → no, proven money + sized locks + alerts. “What if it loses?” → public grade, can’t hide, trial/cancel.

**Secret-shop note:** read our own paywall/sales path as a stranger — does it sell membership features or current→desired?
